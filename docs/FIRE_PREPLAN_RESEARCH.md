# Fire preplan and scenario rehearsal: research and decision record

## Problem and proposed user

The user is a fire department preincident planning officer or company officer preparing for a complex commercial property. USFA says a preincident plan collects facility arrangement, apparatus access, water supply, construction, contents, and protection-system locations, and must be retrievable and current at the incident. NIST says indoor information is often limited to a 2D blueprint, when one exists, and that producing structured indoor data manually does not scale. The opportunity is to turn a partial scan and exterior imagery into a **reviewable preplan and rehearsal scenario**, with source and freshness attached to each fact. The incident commander still makes the live decision from observed conditions and department procedure.

Sources: [USFA preincident planning](https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/ax/sm/sm_r0227.pdf), [USFA information management](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/risk_management_practices.pdf), [NIST 2026 building intelligence](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/building-intelligence-first-responders-foundation-models-interoperable).

## Track fit and product shape

The CoStar prompt supplied by the team asks for at least two data layers, spatial understanding, an acting agent, and a Monday-useful result. Use Matterport for *verified scan coverage and indoor geometry*, Vexcel for exterior site context, and CoStar for property identity and available building metadata. A model covering one suite must never be represented as the whole property. The Amazon prompt rewards a working, adaptive, usable agent; a crew can correct an exit, block a route, or change a scenario and see the preplan and exercise update.

**MVP:** Select one verified commercial building with useful scan coverage. Show an aerial/site view and a clearly bounded scanned interior. The agent extracts candidate access points and spatial obstacles, asks a human to confirm critical facts, creates a short preplan card, then generates two *training* scenarios (different hypothetical fire origins or one unavailable route). Simulate occupants and crew tasks on the known geometry, and compare route availability, bottlenecks, search coverage, and task timing ranges. Unknown floors, door operability, hydrants, standpipes, hazardous contents, fire protection, and occupancy are explicit unknowns. Do not output a live attack order or a single optimal entry point.

## Skeptical questions and researched answers

| Challenge | Answer and consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Aren't preplans already a thing? | Yes. NFPA 1620 covers the preplanning process, and NIST is actively testing scan-to-structured-data automation. The differentiated experiment is a joined indoor/outdoor/property record plus repeatable crew-reviewed scenario rehearsal, not the invention of preplans. [NFPA](https://www.nfpa.org/es/product/id/p1620code), [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/building-intelligence-first-responders-foundation-models-interoperable). |
| Why would a firefighter trust a real-estate scan? | They should not trust it unconditionally. The sponsor-data field note reports partial Matterport coverage and sparse sprinkler fields. Record coverage, capture date, source, and human verification, and leave operationally important facts unknown until checked. [USFA plan maintenance](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/risk_management_practices.pdf). |
| Could this recommend a dangerous entry? | Yes if presented as an optimizer for a live fire. USFA says the first officer's size-up weighs current fire stage, location, resources, and life-safety possibilities, and revises assumptions as evidence arrives. Restrict the MVP to planning and training hypotheses. [USFA risk management](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/risk_management_practices.pdf). |
| Can a scan predict fire and smoke accurately? | No. Fuel properties, ventilation, door state, suppression, construction, and ignition are not recoverable from geometry alone. NIST's FDS is a validated engineering framework for specified scenarios, but its applicability and uncertainty need evaluation. For the demo, use clearly hypothetical hazard zones and sensitivity ranges, not casualty or tenability predictions. [NIST FDS](https://pages.nist.gov/fds/manuals.html), [NIST model validation](https://www.nist.gov/publications/verification-and-validation-process-fire-model). |
| Why animate people if the behavior is guessed? | To expose route conflicts and uncertainty. NIST distinguishes movement-only models from behavioral ones and warns that defaults can be optimistic. Show multiple parameter sets and no precise casualty estimates. [NIST model review](https://www.nist.gov/publications/review-building-evacuation-models-2nd-edition), [NIST defaults](https://www.nist.gov/publications/bounding-defaults-egress-models). |
| Does the scan include every exit and all floors? | Often no; door openings do not establish exterior egress or operability. Label the modeled footprint and require confirmed exits. A missing section invalidates route claims about that section. [NIST indoor data gap](https://www.nist.gov/news-events/news/2026/11/building-intelligence-first-responders-foundation-models-interoperable). |
| Would a department use this on Monday? | A short, verified preplan with access, hazards, uncertainty, and a training exercise fits an existing workflow. Test usefulness with a company officer: can they catch a meaningful issue or prepare an exercise faster than with their current plan? USFA explicitly values retrieval, maintenance, and training with preplans. [USFA](https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/ax/sm/sm_r0227.pdf). |
| What counts as success in the demo? | A reviewer can correct a wrong assumption, and the agent reruns the same scenario to show changed route accessibility or task coverage. The comparison should be reproducible with a fixed random seed and disclose every assumed input. |

## Occupant model for a first simulation

Use behavior *parameters and states*, not fixed personality stereotypes. States: **unaware → perceives cue → interprets / seeks information → prepares or helps → moves toward selected exit → reroutes or exits**. NIST documents delay, information seeking, helping, familiarity with routes, group influence, and differing mobility. Suggested scenario knobs are cue strength and alarm delay, familiarity with the main entrance, paired/group movement, willingness to assist, walking speed, and route blockage. Sample several runs over parameter ranges; keep the same seed when comparing geometry. Avoid a universal “panic” character: NIST reports that people often act rationally and help others.

Sources: [NIST behavioral findings](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=861543), [NIST evacuation decision model](https://www.nist.gov/publications/evacuation-decision-model), [NIST mobility and social influence](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/TechnicalNotes/NIST.TN.1825.pdf), [NIST pre-evacuation data](https://www.nist.gov/publications/pre-evacuation-database-use-egress-simulations).

## Firefighter model for a first simulation

Model **crew tasks and decision gates**, not firefighters as shortest-path blobs. A training scenario begins with arrival and size-up; the officer confirms reported fire location, visible conditions, possible occupants, access, resources, and a department-defined objective. Crew agents remain together, enter only under the scenario's approved condition, traverse known routes with an assigned search or rescue task, report findings, and change course when the assumed route becomes unavailable. Parameterize task time with local drill data. FSRI provides time-to-task drill sheets and observed victim-removal speed ranges; its training drills focus on entry point selection, isolation, ventilation, suppression coordination, and victim removal. These experimental findings are mainly residential and should not be treated as calibrated commercial-building tactics.

Sources: [USFA incident risk management](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/publications/risk_management_practices.pdf), [FSRI search drills](https://training.fsri.org/resources/folder/20/search-and-rescue-drills), [FSRI time-to-task](https://fsri.org/research-update/conduct-your-own-time-task-drills-search-rescue), [FSRI experiments](https://fsri.org/research-update/report-series-and-tactical-considerations-released-analysis-search-and-rescue).

## Build decision

First implement a sourced preplan and route-availability rehearsal on one verified site. Add behavior states and task timing ranges only after the floor, exits, and exterior access are verified. A sophisticated fire spread model or operational strategy ranking requires materially more inputs and validation than the hackathon dataset and present code provide.
